# CXA-010 Eight residual-risk monitors
Item: CXA-010 (ch. 22) · Lineage: none (monitors read, they take no look) · Market: AU · Status: monitors 1 to 8 built

## Hypothesis
Eight standing risks of the Cortex are watched once a quarter by read-only monitors: demotion-worthy loss in smaller companies, the operating point of the family test, over-stated confidence, two-basis stops, a family judged in one regime, the disagreement rule, stalled trades and the share of setups the fill model leaves out. A monitor writes its own row and, when a line is crossed, one plain Owner item. It changes no family status, weight, threshold, rule, book or order.

## Pre-registration
None: a monitor is a measurement, not a test, and takes no look. Decisions D-1614 to D-1622 (design), D-1675 to D-1679 (monitors 1 to 4, task M1) and D-1700 to D-1703 (monitors 5 to 8, task M2) in docs/M23_design.md. [PROPOSED]: the 90 % one-regime share (D-1619), still open.

## Data
Source: production tables (Cortex decisions, outcomes, cohorts, regime days, calibration looks); no history
Window: one quarter, computed on the first night after the quarter's last session
Universe: the home market, per family where the monitor is per family
Known-time rules: a row is written once per monitor, market and period (`INSERT OR IGNORE`); a quarter that is not closed has no row
Caveats:
- the first quarter (2026-Q4) is not yet closed, so no quarterly row exists; the calibration rows of monitors 2 and 6 are the only one-off rows
- monitor 7 has no stored expected stall rate, so its expected value is the shadow arm's rate over the four earlier quarters, labelled so (D-1702)
- monitor 5 needs at least 30 setups of the family in the quarter before it labels, and the share is compared in whole counts (D-1700)
- monitor 8 counts the fill model's own `unfilled` outcome; `cancelled` is left out of both counts, and the alarm needs 20 scored setups in each quarter (D-1703)

## Statistic
Monitor 1 hit rate and the one-sided 90 % bootstrap bound of event entries in smaller companies minus large ones; 2 simulated false- and true-pass rates; 3 the 90 % upper bound of the calibration slope (alarm below 0.7); 4 count of two-basis stops and of those that closed; 5 regime share; 6 a record row and a revisit trigger; 7 realised against expected stall rate (alarm at 5 points); 8 rise in the unfilled share (alarm at 5 points). Whole-basis-point comparison, so exactly 5 points fires.
